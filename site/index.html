An acoustic anomaly detector in six 30 × 30 tiles

The MLPerf Tiny anomaly detector needs 380 tiles. I compressed it to 6, one per layer, and simulated it on a 6-bit analog MAC array with no A/D between hidden layers. It keeps of the full model's margin over chance, and the analog hardware adds no measurable loss, provided the model is trained for the chip.

Three findings

Most important first. The numbers mark where each one sits in the signal chain.

  1. 1

    No A/D between hidden layers holds up

    With fixed-range converters, dropping the 10 inner conversions costs nothing measurable (+0.007 ± 0.008 AUC). The limit is the analog signal path: both designs fail together once read noise nears 1 LSB.

    For the chipSpecify signal-path noise, not converter placement.

  2. 2

    Training, not tighter programming, decides yield

    At 3 % weight-programming error, chips trained the usual way fall below chance. Trained with the chip's errors simulated, none do, and AUC stays at up to 3 %.

    For the chipA 3 % program-verify tolerance is enough, if the training recipe ships with the weights.

  3. 3

    Weights need 4 bits; the converters set the floor

    4-bit weights cost 0.010 AUC and 4-bit biases nothing. The D/A and A/D need 5 bits, or 4 with a per-input offset subtracted before the D/A: every precision at 4 bits then scores 0.723 against 0.722 at 6.

    For the chip4-bit cells instead of 6-bit. Converter bits hinge on one front-end feature.

Open questions

Three things only the silicon can settle

Questions for the hardware team

  1. Is the analog read noise a fixed floor, or proportional to the signal? It sets the noise budget, and decides whether removing the inter-layer converters stays free (proportional noise costs 0.016).
  2. Is subtracting a per-input offset before the D/A cheap in your front end? It decides whether the converters can drop from 6 to 4 bits.
  3. How many 30 × 30 arrays are on a die, and can one drive the next directly in analog? The 6-tile design depends on it.

What this does not show

  • Simulation only. Noise levels are assumptions or stress tests, not measurements of a real chip.
  • One modest benchmark. At 10 % false alarms the detector catches about 37 % of ToyCar faults. The relative costs above should transfer; the absolute numbers belong to this dataset.
  • Not modelled: power, temperature, weight drift, gain and offset errors in the analog path, and a front end simulated from the raw waveform.
  • Statistics: 3 to 4 training runs per condition; differences below about 0.02 are not claimed unless consistent across runs.
